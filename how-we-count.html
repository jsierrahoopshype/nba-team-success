How we count

The definitions behind every number in Team Success, in plain words.

Seasons and teams

A season is shown as "1996-97". Every team appears under the name it had that season (the 1955-56 Rochester Royals, not today's Sacramento Kings). Team names link to that franchise's Career Map page; teams that no longer exist (the Anderson Packers, the Chicago Stags and others from the 1940s and 50s) have no page, so their names are not links.

Records

Win % is wins divided by wins plus losses. A team's record, finish and playoff result come from our standings table. "Rank" is our own season ranking of all teams: the champion is 1, the runner-up 2, then by playoff round reached and win %.

A "60-win season" means a 60-win pace: a win % of at least 60/82 (.732), so shortened seasons (1998-99, 2011-12, 2019-20) and the shorter schedules of the 1940s to 60s count fairly. The worst or best record of a season is the lowest or highest win %; when teams tie, every tied team counts.

Was he on that team?

A player was on a team-season if he played at least one regular-season game for it. Every count shows his games for that team next to it, and every page and list has a "20+ games only" filter.

"Played" means he logged minutes or any stat. Minutes were not kept in 1946-47 to 1950-51, and only in some games from 1951-52 to 1975-76; in a game without minutes, being in the box score counts as playing. From 1996-97, a player with 0 minutes and no stats who was on the floor in the lineup data counts too: those are seconds-long appearances, not DNPs.

With him and without him

For each player and team-season: the team's record in the games he played vs the games he missed, inside his window with that team. With one team all season, the window is the whole season. If he changed teams, the window with the first team runs from the start of the season to the day before his first game for the next team, and the next window starts the day after his last game for the previous team. The career numbers add up every window. The gap is win % with him minus win % without him.

Two limits: we cannot see the day a player was waived or traded, only the games he played, so the days between his last game for one team and his first for the next count for both. And a player cut in mid-season with no new team keeps a window to season's end, so those team games count as "without him".

The playoff numbers work the same way, shown separately, for each team he played at least one playoff game for.

On/off, starts and close games (1996-97 on)

These come from Ramiro Bentes' public lineup and possession data, which starts in 1996-97. Before that they show "n/a".

  • On/off per 100: the team's net points per 100 possessions with him on the floor minus with him off the floor, in the games he played for that team.
  • Started / bench: the team's record in games he was in the first five vs games he came off the bench.
  • Close games: games where, at some point in the last 5 minutes of the 4th quarter or overtime, the margin was 5 points or fewer, and he was on the floor for at least one of those possessions. Shows the team's record in those games.

Before 1996-97, minutes are often missing, so the with/without records count games only.

Lists

Every list covers regular seasons 1946-47 through 2025-26 (the current season is only on the daily boards) and has three eras: all-time, since 1976-77 and since 1996-97. "Winning seasons" and "playoff droughts" count the seasons he played, in a row; a season he missed entirely neither breaks nor extends a streak. For a season split between teams, a winning season uses the team he played the most games for, and a playoff trip means he played a playoff game or the team he finished with made the playoffs.

The Accolade score uses the Teammates Score weights; the formula is on the Bad Luck Index page.

What is missing or corrected

  • One 1953-54 Milwaukee Hawks vs Baltimore Bullets game is missing from the box scores, and three games from 1946-47, 1950-51 and 1952-53 have results that disagree with the official standings. Team records use the official standings; with/without uses the games we have.
  • Eight tiebreaker games from 1948 to 1957 and the cancelled April 2013 Celtics-Pacers game are left out of the regular season.
  • 2025-26 conference and division finishes are worked out from the records.
  • The current season (2026-27) uses Ramiro's daily lineup data until the box-score archive adds it.
  • Seasons with no minutes at all: 1946-47, 1947-48, 1948-49, 1949-50, 1950-51.

Sources

Box scores for every game since 1946-47 (Alberto de Roa's nba-boxscores), lineups and possessions since 1996-97 (Ramiro Bentes' nba_data), player names and awards (HoopsMatic's player database), team standings (HoopsMatic). All public.
