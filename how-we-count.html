    How we count

    The rules behind every number in Team Success, traced to the data and code that produce them.

    What each page covers

    Career records (W–L, win %, W − L, with and without) count every regular season from 1946-47 through 2026-27, with 2026-27 games through Nov. 19, 2026, the last game in the nightly data; the nightly job recomputes them. Season counts and finishes (winning seasons, playoff trips, titles, final NBA finish, teammate help) use completed seasons, 1946-47 through 2025-26, because a season in progress has no final finish. The career boards have a "Completed seasons only" option.

    Ranks and scopes

    Every rank on the site (player cards, boards, the daily list, fun facts, share-card data) comes from the board's own rule: its default view, its qualifying group and standard competition ranking, where equal values share a rank shown as "T-155" and the next rank skips ahead. Each career number says its scope in a few words: "incl. 2026-27 to date" for career records (W–L, win %, W − L, with and without), "through 2025-26" for season counts, finishes and teammate help. A link always opens the board in the scope it quotes.

    Formats

    Records use an en dash (706–366). Win rates read 65.9%. With and without is shown as the two win rates, "65.9% with him · 39.9% without", and the gap as "26.0 points higher": the difference between the two win percentages (percentage points), worked out from the unrounded rates. It is not a relative change: +26.0 does not mean 26% more wins. Possession-based on/off is in points per 100 possessions, a different unit, and is always labeled that way. An empty sample (no games) shows N/A, never 0.0%.

    Final NBA finish

    Each team-season's final finish is the RNK column of our standings table. The champion is #1 and the runner-up #2; the other teams follow by playoff round reached, then by regular-season win %. Teams with the same round and win % share a rank. We re-derived every RNK from that rule: 0 of 1,723 team-seasons differ, and 365 share a rank.

    A finish always comes with that season's league size: "#8 of 30". League sizes changed a lot (1946-47: 11, 1949-50: 17, 1950-51: 11, 1953-54: 9, 1961-62: 9, 1966-67: 10, 1967-68: 12, 1976-77: 22, 1980-81: 23, 1988-89: 25, 1995-96: 29, 2004-05: 30; 30 since 2004-05), so the league-size-adjusted finish puts every season on one scale: (rank − 1) / (teams − 1), where 0 is the champion and 1 is last. Average final finish is shown on that scale by default, as a 30-team equivalent: 1 + adjusted × 29, one decimal ("4.1 of 30, adjusted for league size"), so 3rd of 9 teams in 1964-65 counts as 8.25 of 30, not 3rd. The raw average of the ranks is the second view on the board and the small line on the player card. Live standings and conference seeds are labeled as such and never mixed with the final finish.

    Was he on that team?

    A player was on a team-season if he played at least one regular-season game for it. "Played" means he logged minutes or any stat. Minutes were not kept from 1946-47 to 1950-51 and only in some games until 1975-76; in a game without minutes, being in the box score counts as playing. From 1996-97, a 0-minute row with no stats counts when the lineup data puts him on the floor (a seconds-long appearance, not a DNP).

    We removed 252 appearances a player could not have made: source box scores that file one player's line under another player's ID, or on the wrong team (for example, 1979-80 rows that put Dwight Jones on the Houston Rockets; 1950-51 rows that put George Mikan on the Washington Capitols and Rochester Royals). The rule flags games for one team that sit between two games he played for another team with no game of that team missed in between, appearances for two teams on the same day, and stints made only of 0-minute rows in games where minutes were kept. Among the flagged rows, the 0-minute ones go; when there are none (seasons without minutes), the rows of the team he played fewer games for that season go. Every removed row is listed below.

    With him and without him

    "With him" is the team's record in the regular-season games he played for it. "Without him" is the team's record in games he missed, and it needs a window: which missed games count. No free source we are allowed to use has historical signing, waiver or trade dates, so we publish two definitions, both rebuilt from the game logs.

    • Team-season window (the headline number): with one team all season, the whole season. If he changed teams, the first window ends the day before his first game for the next team, and the next window starts the day after his last game for the previous one.
    • Appearance span: only the team's games between his first and last game for that team (per stint). No game before he arrived or after he left counts, and no game counts for two teams. It can miss a real absence at the start or end of a stint.

    What the window proxy counts that the span does not, across every player: 127,111 team games before a one-team player's first appearance (late signings, call-ups, injuries) in 6,734 team-seasons; 154,161 games after his last appearance (cuts, season-ending injuries) in 7,444 team-seasons; and 48,524 games in the days between two teams, which count for both, in 5,185 traded team-seasons.

    The gap is win % with him minus win % without him, in percentage points. Leaderboards that rank gaps set separate minimums for games played and games missed and show both samples.

    Worked example: Michael Jordan

    With him: 706–366 in 1,072 games (65.9%), wins minus losses +340. Without him, team-season window: 63–95 in 158 games (39.9%), 26.0 points higher with him. Without him, appearance span: 26–59 in 85 games (30.6%), 35.3 points higher with him.

    The difference is mostly 1994-95. He played only for the Chicago Bulls that season, so the window is the whole season and the team's 34–31 before his first game on March 19, 1995 counts as "without him". The span starts at his first game, so those 65 games drop out. Seasons with no games for any team (1993-94, 1998-99, 1999-00, 2000-01) are outside both definitions: no team-season, no window.

    Seasons split between teams

    Each stint is its own row with its own window. Where a summary needs one row per season (winning seasons, average finish, streaks), it uses the stint with the most games; ties go to the later team. A playoff trip means he played a playoff game, or the team he finished the season with made the playoffs; a title follows the same rule. Play-in losses are not playoff trips.

    Playoffs

    Playoff with/without works the same way, shown separately, for each team he played at least one playoff game for. A team's playoff result always comes from the standings; series scores come from the playoff games. Play-in games are in neither the regular season nor the playoffs. In 1953-54 the first round was a round robin: the Fort Wayne Pistons and New York Knicks played playoff games but are listed as missing the playoffs, as in the standings.

    On/off, starts and close games (1996-97 on)

    • On/off per 100: the team's net points per 100 possessions with him on the floor minus with him off, in the games he played for that team. A different unit from the win-rate gaps.
    • Started / bench: the team's record in games he was in the first five vs games he came off the bench.
    • Close games: games where the margin was 5 points or fewer at some point in the last 5 minutes of the 4th quarter or overtime, and he was on the floor for at least one of those possessions.

    These come from Ramiro Bentes' public lineup and possession data, which starts in 1996-97. Before that they show N/A.

    Lists

    Every list covers regular seasons 1946-47 through 2025-26 and has three eras: all-time, since 1976-77 and since 1996-97. "Winning seasons" and "playoff droughts" count the seasons he played, in a row; a season he missed entirely neither breaks nor extends a streak. The Accolade score uses the Teammates Score weights; the formula is on the Bad Luck Index page.

    Fun facts, the daily story and the comparison of the day

    Every sentence is a template filled from the nightly data, never written by a model. The nightly job runs four times a day; the first run of a date writes that day's fun facts and story, and a later run rewrites them only when newer games arrive. Fun facts (6 to 8 a day) come from six kinds of templates: a player who passed another on the career wins or W − L list in the last game date's games; a team's best or worst start through that many games since a given season; a team unbeaten or winless in the games a player played or missed (only with 10+ games played and 8+ missed); tonight's games where an active player has a record of .700+ or .350 or lower against the opponent (20+ games); winning-season streaks and playoff droughts still alive; and title-winning or 60-win-pace teams on this date. Each links to the row that proves it. The same fact never appears two days running, and a streak or on-this-day fact never repeats within 30 days.

    Last night in Team Success lists 4 to 10 noteworthy items from the last game date's games: career wins and W − L milestones, a move into the top 25 in career win % (500+ games), passes of notable players (top 100 on that board or a 2+ time All-Star, by players in that board's top 250), franchise-best or worst starts and streaks, with and without splits with 10+ games played and 8+ missed, and notable records against tonight's opponent (15+ games). A night with nothing noteworthy says "Quiet night" and shows three items labeled "From the archive"; when no new games have arrived, the list says which games it covers. The comparison of the day pairs two players from the same era with 300+ games each, at least one of them an All-Star, from a pool of 120 pairs that rotates daily (no pair repeats within 120 days).

    What is missing or corrected

    • One 1953-54 Milwaukee Hawks vs Baltimore Bullets game is missing from the box scores, and eight games from 1946-47 to 1953-54 have results that disagree with the official standings. Team records use the standings; with/without uses the games we have.
    • Eight tiebreaker games from 1948 to 1957 and the cancelled April 2013 Celtics-Pacers game are left out of the regular season.
    • Ten neutral-site games (2024-25 and 2025-26) have the missing team and score filled in from the box score.
    • 2025-26 conference and division finishes are worked out from the records.
    • Seasons with no minutes at all: 1946-47, 1947-48, 1948-49, 1949-50, 1950-51.
    • The current season uses Ramiro's daily lineup data until the box-score archive adds it.
