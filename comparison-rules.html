How teammate help and peer comparisons work

    The rules behind "Teammate help", "Compare with a peer" and the cohort lists.

    Teammate help

    Help uses the HoopsMatic Teammates Score weights: MVP and Finals MVP 10; All-NBA First Team and Defensive Player of the Year 4; All-NBA Second Team 3; All-NBA Third Team 2; All-Star and All-Defensive First Team 1; Rookie of the Year, Sixth Man, All-Defensive Second Team and All-Star MVP 0.5; Most Improved and All-Rookie First Team 0.25; All-Rookie Second Team 0.125. An MVP already covers that season's All-NBA nod and a DPOY that season's All-Defensive nod.

    A season's help adds up the honors won that season by the teammates who shared at least one regular-season game with him for that team. We check every game, so a player traded in February gets only the teammates he played with. Players are joined on their NBA person ID, team and season.

    Zero and missing are different. 0 means no teammate he played with was honored. A "+" after a number means a teammate with 10 or more shared games has no award record in our player database (his name did not match), so the true number may be higher.

    Per season, not career totals. A player's help is the average over his seasons (one team per season, the one he played the most games for). A long career does not raise it.

    Entering the season. Same-season honors can reflect the winning they are being compared against: a 60-win team gets more All-Stars. "Entering" uses the same teammates' honors from the season before, which the season's results cannot have influenced.

    Suggested peers

    Every player with 300 or more career games gets three suggested peers, chosen from the same pool by four measures: era (the game-weighted middle of the career, within 4 seasons), help (average per season, within a log distance of 0.35), his own honors per season (within 0.5) and career length (games, within a log distance of 0.7). The three with the smallest combined distance (era / 4 + help / 0.35 + career length / 0.7 + honors / 0.5) are shown. When fewer than three pass those limits, the closest players within 6 seasons of era fill the list and say so.

    Each suggestion states the numbers it was matched on. Comparisons align the two careers by career season (season 1 with season 1), so players from different eras can be compared the same way. A comparison states records and finishes, not what a player caused.

    How the head-to-head scoreboard works

    • Categories. Career record (win % in his games), W − L, titles, playoff trips, deepest run, average final finish (adjusted for league size, on a 30-team scale), with vs without, and help per season.
    • The leader's cell is tinted: the larger number, except average finish and help, where the smaller one leads (a better finish is a lower number; less help means he won with fewer honored teammates).
    • With vs without is shown, never counted. It is the win-rate gap in percentage points between the games he played and missed, for reference only.
    • The tally ("Jordan leads 5 of 7") counts the seven lead categories; equal values, or a missing value on one side, count as even.
    • Less help names the player whose teammates were honored less, with both averages: help is the Teammates Score of the teammates he shared games with, per season.
    • These are team records and finishes. They do not measure who was the better player.

    Compare any two players: pick both sides; the same scoreboard and season table are built in the page from each player's data file.

    Cohort lists

    • Best finishes among low-help All-Stars: All-Stars with 300+ games and 5+ seasons whose average help is in the lowest quarter of that group, ranked by average final finish adjusted for league size (30-team scale, raw average alongside); a second table uses help entering the season.
    • Same help, different results: pairs of players from the same era (career middles within 4 seasons) with help within 15% of each other and 500+ games each, ranked by the difference in average final finish.
    • On every player page: his best and worst final finish among the seasons with help within 25% of his median.

    Share images

    Each player's three suggested comparisons (players with 300+ games) have their own page. Every comparison, suggested or picked on the compare page, and every player has a social card in the Player Comparison card style; "Download image" saves that same card.
